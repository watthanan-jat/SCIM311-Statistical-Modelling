Weeks 10–17

Part II overview

Part II of SCIM311 focuses on time-series analysis: how observations depend on time, how to describe that dependence, and how to forecast future values.

We start with plots, stationarity, autocorrelation, and the ACF and PACF. We then study MA, AR, ARMA, ARIMA, and SARIMA models, using the Box–Jenkins workflow to identify, fit, check, and use models in R.

Weeks 10–13

Foundations and stationary models

Time-series structure, stationarity, ACF/PACF, and MA, AR, and ARMA models.

Weeks 14–16

Modelling and forecasting

Differencing, ARIMA, Box–Jenkins diagnostics, SARIMA, and forecasts.

What students will achieve

Part II learning outcomes

These are the two course learning outcomes assessed in Part II.

CLO3

Analyse stationary time series

Explain time-series concepts and analyse stationarity, autocorrelation, ACF/PACF, and MA, AR, and ARMA models.

CLO4

Diagnose and forecast

Forecast time-series outcomes using ARIMA/SARIMA models and the Box–Jenkins model-building and diagnostic workflow.

45% of the course grade

Part II assessment

The four components below add up to 45% of the overall course grade.

30%

Final exam

A written exam covering the Part II time-series material.

5%

R coding assignments

Two assignments require you to write and submit your own R code. Assignment 1 is released in Week 12 and Assignment 2 in Week 15. An automatic grader checks whether your code produces the required results, so follow each assignment’s instructions carefully.

5%

Quizzes

The paper-based quiz in Week 14 tests your understanding of concepts covered in class and in the assignments. Bring a pen.

5%

In-class activity

Work with a partner on short pen-and-paper activities that practise the week’s time-series ideas. Submit one answer sheet per pair during class. Each activity is recorded as submitted or not submitted; answers are not graded for correctness.

Assignment collaboration policy

  • Discussing ideas and approaches with classmates is encouraged.
  • Each student must write and submit their own solution separately.
  • Do not share solutions or copy another student’s work.
  • Plagiarism receives a mark of zero for that work.
